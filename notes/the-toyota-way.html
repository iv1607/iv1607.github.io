Work & systems

The Toyota Way

This is one of the most impactful books to read if you work in any type of corporate or manufacturing environment. The focus is that lasting improvement comes from a long-term philosophy, a visible process, capable people, and relentless learning.

2 min readBook notes

Jeffrey Liker organizes Toyota’s management system around 14 principles, but I remember it more simply as four connected ideas:

Purpose→Process→People→Problem solving

What sticks with me

  • Think beyond the quarter. Make decisions that strengthen the system, even when the immediate result is less convenient.
  • Make problems visible. Flow, pull, standard work, and visual controls expose abnormalities early. A hidden problem cannot be improved.
  • Eliminate waste. Look beyond defects to overproduction, waiting, transport, overprocessing, inventory, motion, and unused human creativity.
  • Build people, not dependence. Leaders teach, teams own the work, and suppliers are challenged with respect.
  • Go see and learn. Visit the actual place, understand the actual condition, test a countermeasure, reflect, and repeat.
  • Practice disciplined problem solving. Slow down long enough to clarify the real problem, test causes with facts, follow countermeasures through, and preserve what works.

Practical problem solving

  1. 01Clarify the problem
  2. 02Break down the problem
  3. 03Set a target
  4. 04Analyze root cause
  5. 05Develop countermeasures
  6. 06See them through
  7. 07Monitor results & process
  8. 08Standardize success

Standard work is not the finish line. It is today’s best-known starting point for tomorrow’s improvement.

Most companies I've worked for focus on tools and blindly implementing the tools outlined in the book. However, the true power of the Toyota Production System is the culture and mentality. Focus on long-term thinking, develop your people, and continuously strive to do better. That's what most organizations fail to grasp.
